Summer 2026 Anime Season

  Today, I just want to go over some of the anime I've been watching for the summer 2026 season. There's a lot I was interested in this season! Honestly, maybe too many. I don't know if I'll be able to juggle 5 weekly shows once school starts back up, but it's nice to have options! I can sort of tell already which ones will be first on the chopping block.

Jaadugar: A Witch in Mongolia

  I spoke about it in my first post, but Jaadugar is definitely my favorite of the season so far. The location and time it is set in is pretty interesting and VERY unique for anime, the art style is beautiful, the animation is amazing at times, and the music complements the visuals really well. I'm starting to realize I really like historical fiction sometimes. I remember having to read historical fiction in middle school and stuff, and, honestly, I never really liked it. I remember liking The Alchemist by Paulo Coelho, but I think I was always sort of turned of by it. Turns out, the books I was forced to read just had boring plots, I guess. I don't really remember much I learned about the Mongolian empire, or really anything from history class save the Battle of the Bulge, but it's been pretty compelling to follow.

  I do have to be honest, though. The way this show frames slavery so far has been a little offputting. At the beginning of the first episode, the main character, Sitara, is sold to the family that ends up raising her. I don't know much about what slavery was like in 13th century Iran, but hot take! Owning another human being is bad! I know, crazy. Maybe the show will tackle it more later on, but Sitara's mistress, Fatima, is never portrayed in a negative light for, like, owning her and her other slaves.

  To play devil's advocate, this show just isn't about the injustices of slavery in the 13th century. It's about the rise of the Mongol empire and how Sitara moves through stuch turbulent times (at least that's what it seems to be about, I'm only 4 episodes in, maybe I'm wrong). I think that would be a fine excuse if the main character's primary motivation didn't revolve around satisfying her former mistress. Sitara is treated pretty well by her mistress. She's treated well, she's educated, she's basically a part of the family. Hell, Fatima dies jumping in front of a sword to save her life. Even if that's the case, though, even if she was treated with empathy and kindness, she was still their property, and the other slaves continue to remind her of that. I can see how being taken care of at such a young age by this woman, especially right after her mother's death, Sitara could form a strong bond with Fatima and feel a special connection and a sense of loyalty to her. I'm probably not the right person to be weighing in on this, and I'm curious what others would have to say.

  Well, I talked about that way longer than I though I would. I've been enjoying the show so far, and from the way things are developing, I'm looking forward to seeing how Sitara works her way up the Mongol Empire. I'm very curious what her real endgame will be. I'd honestly recommend this show to any history nerd. Even if you're not really into anime, I think you could find something to enjoy here!

The Ghost in the Shell

  I've owned a DVD of the original Ghost in the Shell for like 2 years now and I'm still yet to have watched it. I have no way of comparing this new adaptation to the original or the manga, but I have to say, this adaptation has a much different vibe than I had expected from Ghost in the Shell. The original movie always seemed so moody and serious. This adaptation has it's moment's, but it's pretty fun and light at times. I feel like, normally, adaptations of manga don't really change much, but from discussions I see online, each adaptation of Ghost in the Shell differs pretty substantially.

  I don't know why, but when I watched the first episode, it felt kind of... weird? I assumed I was just tired, so I just went to sleep and figured I'd finish it the next day. Well, the weird vibe kinda continued the next day. I think it's because things are just immediately and rabidly HAPPENING, with little to no explanation, which is kinda cool! Maybe this adaptation expects you to already know a good amount, sort of like the beginning of FMA: Brotherhood, but it's kind of interesting having to slowly pick up information based on context and hints. I'm 3 episodes in and I still don't fully understand what a Ghost is, or what the deal with micromachines and androids are. Maybe I just need a rewatch, or maybe this show is more for longtime fans than new viewers. Still, it's really cool and well animated. The opening song is pretty sick. The bit at the end of the chorus is really sick, with all the different scenes they show and the rising feeling or whatever. I don't know what it's called, maybe I'm stupid.

  I've also REALLY been loving this trend of new adaptations of old manga keeping their older art style. Urusei Yatsura is STILL one of my favorite slice-of-life anime, such an amazing new adapatation of a classic manga. I wish they'd made even more instead of skipping chapters. I probably need to go read more Rumiko Takahashi manga, she's such a legend. Anyway, I hope they keep doing stuff like this! I don't know about a ton of classic manga to be honest. The only things I can think of off the top of my head are, like, Fist of the North Star and City Hunter. Those probably haven't have aged as well though.

  It's crazy that Science Saru is putting out Jaadugar and this IN THE SAME SEASON. They really came out of nowhere for me, I hadn't heard of them until they adapted Dandadan. They're a contender for, like, my top 5 favorite anime studios right now (Trigger at #1, you know it).

The Elusive Samurai S2

  This is another great example of good historical fiction anime. I'm not sure how based in reality this story is. Apparently Sitara and a lot of the other characters in Jaadugar are based on a real people. Obviously, I know that Ghengis Khan's sons were real people, and probably their wives to, but I just assumed a character like Sitara would've been fabricated for the story.
